# mml-bookmark — competitor and opportunity research

Research date: **22 October 2026**. Prices are in USD unless explicitly stated. Annual totals are labeled as annual totals; monthly equivalents with annual billing are not monthly subscriptions.

**Yes: close competitors already exist.** Stasht and Sorti closely match the proposed combination of social posts, screenshots, links, recipes, and places. Siloe and Dewey compete on consolidating native social saved libraries; Raindrop.io and mymind compete as general personal libraries. The initial proposition validates an existing problem, but it is not a new category.

This is a focused shortlist of **18 platforms**, not a complete market census. Product capabilities below are documented vendor offerings, not hands-on test results. Public user reviews were read for lived experience; review identities/purchases were not independently authenticated. Reviews are self-selected, geographically mixed, and version-dependent. “Not verified” means this research could not establish a fact, not that the feature does not exist. Marketing comparisons about competitors were not adopted as evidence.

## Platform direction for mml-bookmark

**Confirmed product direction from the user: a browser-accessible application and a mobile app.** The target mobile operating systems have not yet been specified. Supporting both iOS and Android is a recommendation for broad reach, not an already-confirmed scope decision.

Suggested experience:
- A full web app for browsing, searching, and organizing the same saved library used on mobile.
- Mobile capture through the iOS share sheet / Android sharing interface, alongside browsing saved items.
- Consistent categories, saved-content state, and recipe presentation across clients.
- Consider a browser extension for quick saving; this is an additional capture channel rather than the browser app itself.
- Treat separate Windows/macOS/Linux applications as optional; the confirmed browser requirement already provides desktop access.

**Competitive implication (analysis):** web + iOS + Android is already documented for many broad competitors, including Raindrop.io, mymind, Recall, Fabric, Bublup, Karakeep, Linkwarden, Reader, Instapaper, Pinterest, Notion, Are.na, and ReciMe. Distribution across browser and mobile is therefore a baseline to execute well. Anybox has an explicit web/Windows limitation; Siloe explicitly lacks a web app; Dewey's documented phone access is through a browser, with no native mobile app verified here. Sorti's web library and Stasht's exact desktop implementation remain research uncertainties.

## Three different promises to distinguish

1. **Save a URL:** keep a link and perhaps a title/thumbnail. The item can still disappear at its source.
2. **Import existing saved posts:** retrieve the backlog already saved within Instagram, TikTok, X, or another service. This is a different integration from accepting a shared link.
3. **Preserve and understand content:** retain actual text/media where supported, extract OCR/transcripts or structured information, and retrieve it later.

These should be measured separately. For example, [Raindrop's archive documentation](https://help.raindrop.io/web-archive) explicitly excludes embedded video, audio, iframes, and login-only pages. It can retain a YouTube transcript without retaining the video file. By contrast, [Karakeep's documentation](https://docs.karakeep.app/) lists configurable video archiving. Neither statement proves universal social-media coverage.

## Gaps and opportunities for mml-bookmark

The following are **hypotheses to validate**, grounded in the reviewed offerings and complaints. They are not claims that no competitor has these features.

| Priority | Opportunity | Evidence and competitive overlap | What would make the hypothesis credible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make capture dependable and honest | Dewey reviews report missing syncs; Fabric reviews describe failed uploads/login friction; Karakeep has mobile share reports. Archiving limitations are explicitly documented by Raindrop. | Test real Instagram Reels, TikToks, YouTube videos, browser pages, and screenshots on iOS and Android. Show whether each save contains only a link, extracted text, or a retained copy; report failures clearly and allow retries. |
| 2 | Find something from an imperfect memory, with user control | AI search/OCR already exist in mymind, Siloe, Recall, Raindrop, and Karakeep. Sorti reviews describe overly broad or wrong categories for specific topics. | Benchmark queries such as “the pasta with lemon from last month,” including mixed Polish/English captions and speech. Combine meaning-based search with exact words, source/date filters, editable tags, and optional automatic filing. This is an execution test, not a novel feature claim. |
| 3 | Serve one recurring activity exceptionally well | ReciMe proves that recipe-to-grocery-list workflows are an existing proposition. Stasht already handles places/events; Sorti already handles products/recipes/places. | Pick one audience and repeatable activity, such as collaborative household cooking, DIY project references, or trip planning. Establish an unmet detail through interviews and switching tests before expanding to all formats. |
| 4 | Reduce the effort of using saved material | Reminders/resurfacing already exist in Raindrop and Siloe; maps/calendar actions exist in Stasht. Bublup, Are.na, and Notion already support collaboration. | Test whether a short planning view or a shared collection actually increases cooking, visiting, reading, or completing tasks. Measure use of saves, not only the count saved. |
| 5 | Earn long-term trust and make pricing easy to understand | Dewey and mymind reviews show price sensitivity. Bublup's historical plan transition created distrust. Open-source tools and Anybox provide control-oriented alternatives. | Keep clear export/access rules, explain what survives cancellation, and distinguish storage from optional AI/media processing. Offer portable exports and backups as expected capabilities; existing competitors already do this. |

**Multilingual/local opportunity:** a Polish-first experience, strong Polish speech/OCR, metric/imperial handling, and local place matching could be worth testing for a defined audience. This research did not establish an absence of these capabilities across competitors; Recall and Karakeep explicitly advertise multilingual support. Localization would need quality benchmarks and evidence of unmet demand.

**Price pressure:** free Stasht/Sorti, Raindrop's roughly $28 annual US App Store price, and Anybox's $14.99 annual plan create low consumer price anchors. More expensive products sell learning/AI/workspace value. No willingness-to-pay study was performed, so this report does not prescribe a validated mml-bookmark price.

## Suggested validation before implementation

1. Recruit 8–12 people from one likely audience who already save across several apps. Ask them to retrieve recent real saves and watch where the process fails.
2. Benchmark 30–50 representative saves against Stasht, Sorti, Siloe, and Raindrop; add Dewey for large social backlogs and ReciMe for a cooking audience.
3. Measure successful capture, time to save, time to find again, retained content after source changes, and use within a week. Do not substitute vendor feature checklists for these tests.
4. Build a prototype only around the failure users repeatedly encounter and competitors do not handle well enough for that audience.
5. Validate both willingness to switch and willingness to pay. These are separate unknowns.

A defensible initial proposition could be: **“Save useful things from social apps and the web in seconds, then reliably find and use them later.”** It still needs a specific audience and measured advantage; several competitors already use a similar promise.

## Current-status and research qualifications

- **Pocket is historical, not an active competitor.** Mozilla announced its shutdown on 8 July 2025 and the end of export/API access on 8 October 2025. [Mozilla announcement](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/mozilla/building-whats-next/?pubDate=20250522).
- Native saved lists remain a behavioral substitute. For example, [YouTube Watch Later](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/56101?hl=en-See) lets people save videos and Shorts within YouTube. Moving users away from an existing save habit requires less friction or more value.
- **mymind pricing:** the official indexed page was readable, but direct retrieval returned 403. Plan figures above reflect that official indexed evidence, not a completed checkout.
- **Karakeep free limits:** the search index showed an older 10-bookmark/20 MB allowance, whereas the live official page showed 50 bookmarks/100 MB. The live page values are used.
- **ReciMe pricing:** the official gift page specifies $39.99 for a year; the US store lists several Plus offers without consistently identifying their renewal terms. The gift is not represented as a universal regular subscription price.
- **Fabric free access:** free entry is marketed, but the current plans page did not clearly establish a continuing free allowance. Paid annual prices are confirmed from that page.
- **Anybox lifetime pricing:** multiple lifetime prices are displayed across official pages/store offers; the advertised $39.99 offer is not a promise that every buyer receives that price.
- **Reviews:** no star-rating league table is provided because stores, countries, dates, and sample sizes differ. Stasht, Sorti, Siloe, and Linkwarden especially require more direct product testing before drawing strong conclusions.
